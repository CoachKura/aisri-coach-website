# Copyright, trademark and attribution review (team: copyright)

This is a practical publishing risk review. It is not legal advice. Findings are in `reviews/copyright.json`: 14 findings, 0 high, 5 medium, 9 low. Every "old" string occurs exactly once in its file.

## What was checked
- All English `content/*.md` (11 files, about 121k words), the copyright and cover strings in `build.js`, `figures.py` and `figures/figures.json`, and the dossiers in `research/*.md` plus `research/wiki/`.
- **Quotations.** Every `QUOTE:` line (21) and every inline quotation over about 25 characters (about 120) was traced to the dossiers. All real-person quotes appear word-for-word in a dossier with a source. The Kura quotes come from the author's own source text (`src.txt`). All of them are short: the longest is about 30 words (Vijender Singh, Indian Express). That is well within fair-dealing length for criticism and review (Indian Copyright Act s.52(1)(a)).
- **Copied text.** I compared every content line against the 58 Wikipedia texts in `research/wiki` using 9-word n-grams. There were only 5 hits, and each was a short factual phrase (dates and results), not copied prose. I also ran web spot-checks on distinctive sentences, including the VDOT description and the story openings, and found no copied prose.
- **Tables.** No VDOT tables are reproduced. The book says so explicitly (Ch. on VDOT). The E/M/T/I/R table lists only category names and purposes, which are ideas, not expression. The AKURA pace charts in Part 17 are the book's own arithmetic from Riegel's published equation (spot-checked: 15:00 5K gives 31:16 10K). The WBGT table is a simplified example labelled "in the style of" the ACSM flag system, and the ACSM position stand is cited.
- **Trademarks.** VDOT, Garmin Connect, Strava, TCS World 10K, Tata Mumbai Marathon, World Athletics, Athletics Integrity Unit, WADA, USADA, Olympics.com and Reliance Foundation are all used descriptively, with correct names and no implied endorsement.
- **Born to Run.** The book text never mentions the book, McDougall or the Tarahumara, and no passage imitates its text. The phrase "born to run" appears twice (01: l.157, l.243) as the common English idiom, which is fine. Keep all marketing (cover, blurb, ads) free of "in the style of Born to Run" or any similar comparison.
- **Images.** All 36 figures and the cover are generated by the book's own code (`figures.py`, matplotlib; no image imports, logos or photos). Data figures (national-record progressions, gap to world records) are built from public facts. No third-party images were found.
- **Privacy and defamation.** I reviewed the doping passages (Sanjivani Jadhav, Kartik Kumar, Chepngetich, AIU Category A and WADA statistics). All of them are sustained and documented. The pending Abhishek Pal case is correctly left out, and Sunita Rani's overturned case is not raised. The Jaisha/Rio dispute is handled well: her claim, the AFI rebuttal and Kavita Raut's account appear side by side with no verdict. Health details (Sable's ACL, Bains' knee, Lamba's ankle, Athare's cartilage, Jaisha's collapse) are publicly reported sporting injuries. The minors in the book are generic and anonymous. No invented thoughts or dialogue for real people were found.

## Top risks
1. **Doping date conflict (Sanjivani Jadhav).** The book says results were disqualified "from May 2018", but the two dossiers disagree (27 May vs 29 June 2018). Anything said about a named athlete's doping case must match the AIU decision exactly.
2. **Absolute statement about a living person's circumstances.** The book says Jyoti Gawate "never obtained a job", but the only source dates from 2019. It should be dated ("according to 2019 reports").
3. **Sensitive identity detail sourced only to Wikipedia.** Kavita Raut's "tribal family" is stated as fact in 07 (it is hedged in 01). Similar unhedged family details: Preeja Sreedharan's father (09b). Sawan Barwal's parents are named in 01; this is widely reported (HT, News18), so the risk is low, but consider dropping the names of private family members.
4. **Incomplete attribution for the core third-party method.** Riegel's formula drives all the pace charts but is cited nowhere. The Daniels reference has no edition or year, and the three Daniels references disagree on edition.
5. **Copyright-page trademark notice is imprecise (`build.js` `copyright[3]`, not in the JSON because it is not a content file).** It currently reads "VDOT is associated with Jack Daniels and V.O2". VDOT® is a registered trademark of The Run SMART Project, LLC. Suggested replacement:
   > "AKURA, AKURA Endurance Index™, AEI and AISRi are names used by the AKURA Endurance Research System. VDOT® is a registered trademark of The Run SMART Project, LLC. Garmin and Garmin Connect are trademarks of Garmin Ltd. or its subsidiaries; Strava is a trademark of Strava, Inc. TCS World 10K, Tata Mumbai Marathon, World Athletics, Olympics.com and other event, organisation and product names belong to their respective owners and are used only to identify them. This book is independent and is not affiliated with, sponsored or endorsed by any of these owners, or by any athlete, coach or organisation named in it."
   Also add: "All figures and diagrams are original works created for this book. Short quotations from published news reports and interviews are reproduced for the purposes of criticism, review and reporting, with attribution to their source."

Minor points: some pull quotes lack a source (Rawat in 03, Parul in 03), and the Gopi "Ooty helped my performance" quote appears 8 times, often with no named source. Its only source is a blog (shyamgopan.com), so try to find the primary interview. The Kartik Kumar paragraph (06) could be read as implying the Colorado programme was involved. The anaemia-survey reference lacks authors.

## Author checklist before publishing
1. Apply the fixes in `reviews/copyright.json` and replace the `build.js` trademark and credits paragraph as above (the Tamil `strings.json` needs the same change).
2. Confirm the Sanjivani Jadhav dates against the AIU decision, and re-check every doping sentence against the primary notice (AIU/USADA).
3. For every direct quote of a real person, record in a quote log: the exact wording, the primary outlet, the date and the URL. Where a quote reaches us through a secondary source (shyamgopan.com quoting Mid-Day; Olympics.com quoting Inspire Institute; Rediff quoting ANI), try to cite the primary source.
4. Keep quotes short (under about 40 words each) and never reproduce news paragraphs. No permissions are needed at the current lengths. **Permission is needed** if you add longer extracts, any photographs, logos, race posters or screenshots (Garmin/Strava screens, VDOT calculator output), or any table copied from Daniels' Running Formula or the ACSM position stand.
5. Optional courtesy step, recommended for a book that profiles living athletes: send the named living athletes (or their federation or media contact) the passages about them, especially those covering doping, injuries, family deaths, housing or employment. Remove private family names unless they are needed.
6. Do not use athletes' names or likenesses on the cover, blurb or ads without written consent. Doing so implies endorsement and raises personality/publicity-rights risk in India.
7. Do not use "Born to Run", McDougall or the book's cover style in marketing. Keep AKURA's own title.
8. Trademarks: check that "AKURA Endurance Index" does not conflict with existing registered marks before using ™ commercially, and consider filing in India (Class 41/42/9).
9. Keep the "not medical advice" and "not validated" notices on the copyright page and in Appendix A (both are present).
10. Keep `research/` dossiers and the URL lists as the evidence file behind every factual claim about real people.
