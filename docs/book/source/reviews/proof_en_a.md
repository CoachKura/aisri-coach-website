# proof_en_a: English proofreading & spell-check (files 01–04)

Scope: content/01_front_part1.md, 02_part2.md, 03_part3_5.md, 04_part6_7.md (about 2,400 lines).

Method:
- Ran a pyspellchecker pass over every word and triaged about 370 unknown tokens by hand. Most were names, places, URLs or British spellings.
- Grepped for US spellings (-ize/-yze, color, center, toward, etc.), unit and number formats, quote styles, per cent vs %, repeated words, spacing and bracket balance.
- Checked names for consistency across the four files, and against the research dossiers where they disagreed.
- Read all body text in full.

Result: 45 findings. 30 consistency, 9 style, 3 spelling, 2 grammar, 1 attribution. By severity: 1 high, 6 medium, 38 low. Six findings have "new": null because they need a rewrite by the editors.

The spelling is clean overall. It is consistently British (haemoglobin, programme, metres, fuelling, standardise), apart from a single "toward". I found no typos or doubled words.

Top issues:
- HIGH: 03 REFS lists Gore et al. 2013 with the co-author as "Garvican-Hazeltine LA". It should be "Garvican-Lewis LA", which is how 02 already has it.
- The coach's name is spelled two ways: "Surinder Singh Bhandari" in 03 (including the quote) and "Surendra Singh Bhandari" in 04 (and in later files).
- 02: Sable's record progression is described as "almost twenty seconds". The figures in the same file give 20.97 s.
- 03: Ankita Dhyani (9:19.24) is described as "just behind" Parul (9:08.67). The gap is about 10.6 s.
- 01, How to Use: "Parts I to VI" lists only five topics. Heat vs Altitude is missing.
- Repeated passages: the Ch 24 opening nearly duplicates the Part IV story (bus from Mettupalayam). The Chennai Marathon 2026 sentences appear twice in 03. The "born for running" line and the 293 sub-2:10 statistic are repeated in 01.
- Style inconsistencies:
  - 04 uses curly quotes; the rest of the book uses straight quotes.
  - "O.P." vs "O. P." Jaisha.
  - "St. Moritz" vs "St Moritz".
  - "20.9 per cent" vs "20.9%".
  - "5,000 m" vs "5000 m".
  - "pace-HR" vs "HR–pace".
  - "this part" vs "this Part".
  - A few US-style punctuation marks inside quoted terms.
