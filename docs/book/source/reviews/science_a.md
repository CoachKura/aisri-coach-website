# science_a — Science accuracy review (EN, Parts I–VII)

Files: 01_front_part1.md, 02_part2.md, 03_part3_5.md, 04_part6_7.md

What I checked: the Fick equation, CO = HR × SV, the illustrative CO, SV and a–vO₂ ranges, the Hb 1.34 mL O₂/g figure and the 900 g worked example, the 20.9% O₂ fraction, the PB and PIO₂ table (recomputed from the standard atmosphere: all within 1–2 mmHg), dry-air PO₂ (159 and 121 mmHg), "roughly a quarter lower" at 2,000–2,400 m, EPO and reticulocyte time course, RBC lifespan, Gore 2013 (1.1%/100 h), LHTL 1997 (2,500/1,250 m), Chapman 1998, the heat acclimation time course and decay, Lorenzo 2010, sweat-rate formula, WBGT, heat-illness signs and cooling, lactate and MLSS, glycogen stores, CHO 30/60/90 g/h (Jeukendrup 2014), hyponatraemia, fractional utilisation, the VDOT description (fair, no tables copied), and all pace and time arithmetic (Jaisha 3:40/km and 2:49/km; Gulveer 64.8 s/lap; Hari Chand 600+ m; Sawe vs. Sawan about 4 km; record margins). I also checked every REFS entry against the literature.

Result: the science is accurate and well hedged, and safety deferral to clinicians is consistent. There are 9 findings: 0 high, 3 medium, 6 low.

Top issues:
1. The 03 REFS entry for Gore 2013 lists "Garvican-Hazeltine". It should be Garvican-Lewis LA.
2. Heat and Hb mass is stated too absolutely (02 table; 03 ch.31). Rønnestad et al. 2021 (Exp Physiol) found an Hb-mass rise after 5 weeks of heat training.
3. "Rate ratio 1.22 per unit" (03 Kavinadu story) does not name its unit. It is per 1 °C of heat index.
4. The altitude-illness advice (03 ch.24) should add descent when symptoms are severe.
5. Consistency and attribution: Surinder vs Surendra Bhandari, the Hopkins typical-error wording, the Jones "resilience" attribution, and Wong SHS.

All other numbers I could verify were correct: Saltin 1995 79.9 mL/kg/min, the Chatterjee 1991 figure of 43.0, and the anaemia figures, which match the dossier.
