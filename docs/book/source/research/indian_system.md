# The Indian Endurance Running System: Verified Dossier
Compiled 2026-09-29. Each bullet is followed by its source. Anything not confirmed by a retrievable source is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Wikipedia is used only where no primary or news source could be retrieved, and those bullets are labelled "(Wikipedia)". No quotes were invented. The few direct quotes included are reproduced as the cited outlet reported them.

**Corrections to the brief:**
- Nikolai Snesarev died in **March 2021**, not 2024. See section 2.
- I found no evidence that "Renu Kohli" was an Indian distance coach, so that name is dropped.
- The Army steeplechase coach's name is **Amrish Kumar (Adhana)**. One outlet misspells it "Amish".
- The Indian men's marathon record changed in April 2026 (Sawan Barwal). The Indian half-marathon record is disputed. See section 4.

## 1. National camps and altitude

### Ooty (Udhagamandalam), Nilgiris, Tamil Nadu
- Elevation is 2,240 m above mean sea level. Athletes from across India have long gone there for altitude training. [https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/udhagamandalam-boasts-south-indias-only-high-altitude-sports-training-centre/article68333440.ece]
- The Tamil Nadu government opened a dedicated high-altitude training centre at Breeks Ground, Ooty, in **February 2024**. It was inaugurated by CM M.K. Stalin and Sports Minister Udhayanidhi Stalin. It houses 50 athletes selected from government schools, who stay in the SDAT (Sports Development Authority of Tamil Nadu) hostel. The Hindu (June 2024) called it "south India's only high-altitude sports training centre". [same Hindu URL]
- In June 2026 Hindustan Times reported that SAI campuses at Shilaroo (HP) and Ooty (TN) are India's only two high-altitude training centres (HATCs). It gave Ooty's elevation as 2,240 m. A ministry official told HT that the "Ooty centre is a preferred choice for middle and long distance runners". [https://www.hindustantimes.com/sports/others/high-altitude-training-centre-to-come-up-in-shillong-101780588041378.html]
  - Note: The Hindu frames the Ooty HATC as a state (SDAT) centre, while HT describes it as a SAI campus. The ownership and history of any older SAI facility at Ooty are **UNVERIFIED**.
- Lalita Babar, O.P. Jaisha and Sudha Singh reportedly trained together in Ooty under Snesarev before Rio 2016. The source is a secondary outlet, so this is **UNVERIFIED** against a primary source. [https://indianewengland.com/?p=2834]
- The Indian Track Foundation (Karan Singh) moved from Delhi to Ooty and has trained young runners there, including tribal youth from Jharkhand, since 2018. [https://theprint.in/sport/target-olympics-2032-coach-moves-from-gurugram-to-ooty-to-train-runners-from-jharkhand/715322/] [https://www.dnaindia.com/sports/report-in-hilly-ooty-a-running-mission-2782635]

### Shilaroo, Himachal Pradesh (SAI Netaji Subhas HATC)
- SAI press note: a 72-day high-altitude camp for **junior middle- and long-distance runners** ran at Shilaroo from 4 April to 14 June 2022, with 27 athletes and 11 coaches. The centre is at 8,000 ft near Shimla and covers 78 acres. [https://www.sportsauthorityofindia.nic.in/sai/assets/news/1649157667_Middle%20and%20Long%20Distance%20runners%20to%20train%20for%20over%20two%20months%20at%20SAI's%20High%20Altitude%20Training%20Centre%20in%20Shilaroo.pdf]
- HT (2026) gives Shilaroo as 2,450 m and says it "usually hosts high-altitude camps for boxers". [HT Shillong URL above]
- The centre was founded by SAI in 1984 (Wikipedia; not independently confirmed). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhash_High_Altitude_Training_Centre]

### Shillong, Meghalaya (planned)
- On 4 June 2026 HT reported that SAI's Sports Training Centre in Shillong will be upgraded to a HATC at 1,496 m. The cost is ₹150 crore, funded by NSE's CSR programme, for 450+ athletes. No timeline has been set. The plan includes a sports-science building and natural training trails. [HT Shillong URL above]
- The same article quotes a ministry official saying India is "short of roughly 750 coaches". [same]

### Leh, Ladakh
- SAI approved a Khelo India State Centre of Excellence (athletics, boxing, archery) at Spituk Open Stadium, Leh, which sits at about 11,000 ft. [https://ladakh.gov.in/sports-authority-of-india-sai-approves-establishment-of-khelo-india-state-centre-of-excellence-kisce-in-union-territory-of-ladakh/] [https://www.indiasportshub.com/articles/spituk-open-stadium-how-ladakhs-high-altitude-arena-is-redefining-indias-sporting-frontier]
- In May 2025 the Leh Hill Council (LAHDC) and SAI discussed setting up a "High Altitude National Centre for Excellence (HANCE)" in Leh. [https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/high-altitude-national-sports-centre-to-be-set-up-in-leh/]

## 2. Coaching

### Nikolai Snesarev (Belarus)
- Arrived in India in **2005** to coach distance runners. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/nikolai-snesarev-the-late-belarusian-coach-s-contribution-to-indian-athletics]
- Athletes and results:
  - Preeja Sreedharan won 10,000 m gold and Kavita Raut silver at the 2010 Asian Games.
  - He guided Lalita Babar to the Rio 2016 steeplechase final.
  - Olympics.com credits him with Sudha Singh (2010 Asian Games steeplechase gold), O.P. Jaisha and Lalita Babar.
  - [same Olympics.com URL] [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/india-s-athletics-coach-nikolai-snesarev-found-dead-in-hostel-room]
- He left the AFI in **February 2019** "in acrimonious circumstances". One reported factor was Avinash Sable returning to his Army coach. [same URLs; https://www.deccanherald.com/sports/snesarevs-return-leaves-two-indian-2176969]
- He was re-appointed in early 2021, returned to India on 2 March 2021, and was based in Bengaluru.
- He was **found dead on 5 March 2021**, aged 72, in his hostel room at NIS Patiala during Indian Grand Prix 3. The cause was not known at the time and was pending post-mortem. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/india-s-athletics-coach-nikolai-snesarev-found-dead-in-hostel-room] [https://www.espn.com/athletics/story/_/id/31010198/athletics-coach-nikolai-snesarev-found-dead-nis-patiala-hostel-room] [https://indianathletics.in/afi-condoles-sudden-passing-of-coach-nikolai-snesarev/]

### Scott Simmons (USA; American Distance Project, Colorado Springs; also a US Army coach per his X bio)
- AFI appointed him to oversee India's distance running programme. Sportskeeda called him India's distance "head coach" in October 2022. [https://sportskeeda.com/athletics/news-challenging-task-india-s-head-coach-scott-simmons-regroup-distance-running] [https://x.com/adpcoach?lang=en]
- Athletes:
  - Avinash Sable (2022 to about 2024). Simmons is "no longer coaching Sable" per Deccan Herald, Aug 2026.
  - Parul Chaudhary.
  - Gulveer Singh (2024 onward), who won silver in the 10,000 m and bronze in the 5,000 m at the 2026 Commonwealth Games under Simmons.
  - [https://www.deccanherald.com/sports/how-scott-simmons-helped-turn-gulveer-singhs-records-into-cwg-medals-4112643] [https://thebridge.in/athletics/avinash-sable-parul-chaudhary-to-train-in-colorado-springs-45778]
- Exact contract dates are **UNVERIFIED**. One secondary snippet says "until 2024".

  - He moved Avinash Sable into steeplechase around 2017. Sable went back to him after training under Snesarev and broke the national record (8:28.94) at the 2019 Federation Cup.

  - Olympian distance runner (Beijing 2008) and former national record holder at 3000 m and 10,000 m.
  - Reported to be overseeing the national long-distance camp in Bengaluru. The date of that role is **UNVERIFIED**.
  - [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surendra_Singh_(athlete)] [https://www.deccanherald.com/sports/snesarevs-return-leaves-two-indian-2176969]

  - Based in Nashik since 1991, working with SAI and Bhonsala Military School.

- **Yunus Khan (Army):** coaches Harmanjot Singh at Coonoor ("Kunoor" in the source). [https://www.deccanherald.com/sports/tcs-world-10k-triple-delight-for-sanjivani-harman-surprises-domestic-field-3981750]

## 3. Where Indian distance runners come from

### Army / Services
- **Army Sports Institute (ASI), Pune:** set up on 1 July 2001 under the Army's "Mission Olympics" programme. Its Boys Sports Company is a joint SAI–Army venture. [https://armysportsinstitute.com/]
- **Avinash Sable:** born 13 Sep 1994 in Mandwa near Ashti, Beed district (Maharashtra). He enlisted in 5 Mahar Regiment. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-avinash-sable-india-athlete-steeplechase]
- **Gulveer Singh:**
  - Born 1 June 1998 in Uttar Pradesh. He joined the Army in 2018 and holds the rank of Naib Subedar.
  - His breakthrough was 10,000 m bronze at the 2022 Federation Cup.
  - [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-gulveer-singh-india-athlete-long-distance-runner] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulveer_Singh]
- **Rio 2016 marathoners:** all three were Army men.
  - Nitendra Singh Rawat, from Garur, Uttarakhand (joined the Army in 2006).
  - Thonakal Gopi, from Wayanad, Kerala.
  - Kheta Ram, from Barmer, Rajasthan (Jat Regiment).
  - All three qualified at the 2016 Mumbai Marathon.
  - [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitendra_Singh_Rawat] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thonakal_Gopi] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kheta_Ram] [https://www.espn.com/espn/story/_/id/25815512/i-ensure-the-best-marathon-runner-india-says-resurgent-nitendra-rawat-mumbai-win]

### Railways
- **Parul Chaudhary:** born 15 April 1995 in Iklauta near Meerut, Uttar Pradesh. She got a Western Railway sports-quota job in 2015. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parul_Chaudhary] [https://indianathletics.in/athlete/1608-parul-chaudhary/]
- **Ratiram Saini (5,000/10,000 m):** employed by Indian Railways. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratiram_Saini]
- Railways reserve sports-quota vacancies, and the Army, Navy and Air Force recruit sportspersons through special drives. This is the documented job pathway. [https://competition.careers360.com/articles/sports-quota-recruitment] Weak source; I recommend citing a Railway Recruitment Cell sports-quota notice instead. [https://rrccr.com/rrwc/Files/0fab2cae-746f-42bb-aea8-8496e9a89a89.pdf]

### Rural Maharashtra
- **Nashik / Bhonsala Military School:**
  - Kavita Raut (Tungar) is from Nashik. She won a 10,000 m medal at the 2010 Delhi Commonwealth Games. The medal colour is inconsistent across sources (a search snippet said gold; Wikipedia's results list gives bronze), so verify before print. The claim that it was the first Indian CWG athletics medal after Milkha Singh is also **UNVERIFIED**.
  - Sanjivani Jadhav (born 1996, Nashik) switched from wrestling after Vijender Singh persuaded her parents.
  - Monika Athare (born 1992, Nashik) ran the marathon at the 2017 World Championships.
  - Scroll described the group as coming from Nashik's surrounding villages, including tribal communities.
  - [https://scroll.in/field/865878/how-a-former-sai-coach-is-making-nashik-the-hub-for-long-distance-running-in-india] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavita_Raut] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monika_Athare]
- **Satara:** Lalita Babar was born on 2 June 1989 in Mohi village, Satara, into a farming family. She set a 9:19.76 national record in the Rio final and finished 10th. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalita_Babar] [https://sport360.com/article/olympics/189198/lalita-babars-incredible-journey-from-rural-satara-to-3000m-steeplechase-final-at-rio-2016]

## 4. Road races and Indian elite results

### Tata Mumbai Marathon (TMM)
- **History:**
  - First edition 2004; organiser Procam International. The 2021 and 2022 editions were not held.
  - Label history: IAAF Gold Label 2010–12, Silver in 2018, Gold from 2019 (Wikipedia).
  - [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Marathon]
  - The 2026 race was the 21st edition and a World Athletics Gold Label road race. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/mumbai-marathon-2026-race-results-times-winners-list]

  - **Men:** Nitendra Singh Rawat, 2:15:48 (2016). This broke Ram Singh Yadav's 2:16:59 (2012) and qualified him for Rio. In 2019 he ran 2:15:52, missing his own record by 4 seconds. ### Indian national records (distance, as of Sep 2026)
- **Marathon, men:** **Sawan Barwal, 2:11:58**, Rotterdam, 12 April 2026, on his marathon debut. This broke Shivnath Singh's 2:12:00 from Jalandhar in 1978, which had stood for 48 years. T.   - Wikipedia nevertheless lists it as the national record.
  - Sable's 1:00:30 (Delhi 2020) is the prior mark.
  - [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/gulveer-singh-india-athletics-nyc-half-marathon-2026-bronze-national-record] [https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/sports/gulveer-singh-breaks-national-record-at-new-york-city-half-marathon-2026/]

[https://www.olympics.com/en/news/gulveer-singh-india-10000m-national-record-the-ten-2025-athletics-california] [https://indianathletics.in/gulveer-singh-improves-national-10000m-record-narrowly-misses-his-goal-of-sub-27-minutes/]

## 5. Anti-doping context

### WADA Testing Figures (all sports)
- **2024:** India ran 7,113 tests (6,576 urine and 537 blood), with **260 adverse analytical findings, a 3.6% positivity rate**. That was the highest among major testing nations.
- **2023:** 213 AAFs from 5,606 tests, a 3.8% rate.
- **Comparison (2024):** China 24,214 tests at 0.2%; USA 6,592 tests at 1.1%; Russia 10,514 tests with 76 AAFs at 0.7%.
- NADA said the figures reflect "intensified anti-doping efforts".
- [https://www.outlookindia.com/sports/others/india-highest-doping-positivity-rate-wada-2024-testing-report] [https://www.deccanherald.com/india/doping-woes-india-shows-highest-positivity-rate-among-major-nations-in-wadas-2024-testing-report-3834203] [https://thefederal.com/category/sports/india-doping-positivity-rate-wada-2024-testing-report-221209]
- Several outlets describe India as topping the WADA global list for a third straight year. [https://www.thebridgechronicle.com/sports/india-tops-wada-doping-list-2024-report-third-consecutive-year-nada-stats-as99]
- **WADA 2023 ADRV report:** India led all nations with **217 sanctions**. [https://www.thesportsexaminer.com/anti-doping-doping-violations-in-2023-rose-20-over-22-and-up-46-over-21-but-wada-says-this-is-a-positive-sign-india-leads-with-217-sanctions/] Check the original WADA PDF before quoting exact rank tables.

### Athletics-specific (AIU)
- **ADRVs in athletics for India:**
  - 2022: 48 (ranked 2nd).
  - 2023: 63 (2nd).
  - 2024: 71 (1st).
  - 2025: 30 (1st, partial year).
  - [https://www.outlookindia.com/sports/others/world-athletics-integrity-unit-india-extremely-high-risk-classification-update]
- **Category A, 20 April 2026:** the AIU moved the AFI from Category B to Category A ("extremely high" doping risk). This puts India alongside Russia, Belarus, Ethiopia, Kenya, Nigeria and Ukraine. AIU chair David Howman was quoted as saying "the quality of the domestic anti-doping programme is simply not proportionate to the doping risk". [https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/4/20/india-in-extremely-high-doping-risk-bracket-athletics-integrity-unit] [https://thebridge.in/athletics/india-doping-high-risk-aiu-category-a-rules-56360]
- **Category A testing rule:** athletes need at least 3 no-notice out-of-competition tests in the 10 months before Worlds or Olympics, at least 21 days apart. For events from **800 m upward**, these must include at least one ABP blood test and one EPO test. [Outlook URL above]

## 6. Science on Indian endurance athletes (citations)
- **Chatterjee S, Saha SK, Saha D, Nag SK (1991).** "Maximal aerobic capacity of Bengali girl athletes of different sports activities." *Japanese Journal of Physiology* 41:397. doi:10.2170/jjphysiol.41.397. PMID 1960887.
  - 67 Bengali female athletes were tested on a cycle ergometer.
  - Long-distance runners had the highest relative VO₂max, **43.0 ml/kg/min**.
  - For context, Saltin et al. report Kenyan senior runners at about 79.9 ml/kg/min at sea level (see section 7).
- **Nitin YM, Sucharita S, Madhura M, Thomas T, Sandhya TA (2013).** "VO2 max in an Indian population: a study to understand the role of factors determining VO2 max." *Indian J Physiol Pharmacol* 57(2):87–94. PMID 24617157.
  - 20 healthy males aged 18–30. It compares measured VO₂max with Indian prediction equations; the subjects are non-athletes.
  - [https://ijpp.com/IJPP%20archives/2013_57_2_Apr%20-%20Jun/87-94.pdf]
- **Jegatheesan SL, Kumbamoorthy SS, Sanjaykumar S, Rajkumar NCJ (2025).** "Unlocking the connection: hematological status, dietary iron intake and endurance in Indian female athletes." *Physical Activity and Nutrition*. doi:10.20463/pan.2025.0005. PMID 40443248.
  - 104 Tamil Nadu female athletes (volleyball and ball badminton, not runners).
  - **About 53.8% were anaemic.**
- **Background anaemia prevalence in India:** a venous-blood survey in 8 states found anaemia in 44% of adolescent girls, 41% of adult women and 45% of elderly women. [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39779946/]
- **Vora A, Burkule N, Contractor A, Bhargava K (2018).** "Prevention of sudden cardiac death in athletes, sportspersons and marathoners in India." *Indian Heart Journal*. doi:10.1016/j.ihj.2017.12.004. PMID 29455769. It proposes pre-participation screening and medical infrastructure at Indian marathons. [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5903013/]
- **Sickle cell:** a population study in rural and tribal Chamarajanagar (Karnataka) found a sickle allele prevalence of 14.6%. [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12506035/] **No study of sickle-cell trait specifically in Indian athletes was found (UNVERIFIED / gap).**
- **Heat stress:** I found no peer-reviewed heat-stress study of Indian marathons in the time available.
  - A search snippet (not verified against the paper) attributed to one study a Hyderabad Marathon figure: maximum 35 °C, with 252 of 10,000 runners needing medical help. **UNVERIFIED as to citation.**
  - General evidence: a higher heat index raises marathon casualty rates (IRR 1.22 per unit for severe casualties). [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12428376/]
- **Ooty altitude studies:** I found no peer-reviewed study of Indian athletes' haematology at Ooty. **Gap / UNVERIFIED.**
- **National Centre of Sports Science and Research (NCSSR), Indira Gandhi Stadium, New Delhi:**
  - The scheme was formerly the Indian Institute of Sports Sciences and Research (2017–20). About ₹150 crore was budgeted.
  - Minister Mansukh Mandaviya inaugurated it in April 2025.
  - It also supports sports-science departments at 6 universities and sports-medicine departments at 6 medical institutions.
  - [https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1881859&reg=48&lang=2] [https://yas.gov.in/en/sports/national-centre-sports-sciences-and-research-ncssr-0] [https://www.outlookindia.com/sports/mansukh-mandaviya-inaugurates-ncssr-at-indira-gandhi-stadiuminnewdelhi-in-pics] [https://www.theweek.in/news/sports/2021/11/18/sai-to-launch-modern-sports-science-centre-next-year.html]

## 7. Kenya / Ethiopia comparison
- **Iten:**
  - Elevation about **2,400 m**. It calls itself "Home of Champions".
  - It is a **World Athletics Heritage Plaque** site, declared a World Athletics Heritage Landmark in 2019.
  - [https://worldathletics.org/heritage/plaque/list/iten-home-of-champions] [https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/athletics/high-altitude-kenyan-town-iten-draws-olympic-champions-and-amateurs-1.7473288] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iten]
- **Elevation comparison:** Ooty is 2,240 m, Shilaroo about 2,450 m (HT), and Shillong 1,496 m (planned). Ooty is therefore roughly Iten-equivalent. [HT URL above]
- **Sub-2:10 marathon performances** (counts are performances, not unique athletes; data from World Athletics lists compiled by Toni Reavis):
  - 2019: 293 worldwide, of which Kenya 123 (42%), Ethiopia 98, Japan 16, USA 3.
  - 2020: 163 worldwide, of which Ethiopia 54, Japan 34, Kenya 29.
  - 2021: 249 worldwide, of which Kenya 93, Ethiopia 55, Japan 48, USA 1.
  - India: 0, ever. The fastest Indian is 2:11:58.
  - [https://tonireavis.com/2021/12/21/sub-210-marathoning-2021/] Figures for 2022–2025 by country: **UNVERIFIED** (not retrieved). Pull them from the World Athletics top lists if needed.
- **World records:**
  - **Men's marathon:** Sabastian Sawe (KEN) ran **1:59:30** at the London Marathon on 26 April 2026, the first sub-2-hour marathon in a record-eligible race. Yomif Kejelcha (ETH) was second in 1:59:41 and a Ugandan Kiplimo third in 2:00:28 (first name inconsistent across sources). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabastian_Sawe] [https://www.npr.org/2026/04/27/nx-s1-5800113/kenya-sabastian-sawe-east-africa-london-marathon-2-hour-barrier] World Athletics ratification status: **UNVERIFIED**.

The record stands because it predates the positive test. [https://www.espn.com/olympics/trackandfield/story/_/id/46696848/ruth-chepngetich-marathon-world-record-holder-gets-3-year-doping-ban] [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/marathon-world-record-holder-ruth-chepngetich-provisionally-suspended-banned-substance]

  - Saltin B et al. (1995). *Scand J Med Sci Sports*, PMID 7552766. Kenyan runners averaged 68 ml/kg/min at about 2,000 m and **79.9 ml/kg/min at sea level**.
  - Wilber RL, Pitsiladis YP (2012). "Kenyan and Ethiopian distance runners: what makes them so good?" *IJSPP* 7:92. doi:10.1123/ijspp.7.2.92.
