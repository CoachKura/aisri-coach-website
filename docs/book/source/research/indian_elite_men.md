## 2. How the national records changed

### 5000 m
- 13:29.70: Bahadur Prasad (Singh), 27 Jul 1992, AAA Championships, Birmingham. The record stood for about 30 years. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahadur_Prasad (Wikipedia only for the date); Sable broke "the previous mark of 13:29.70 set by Bahadur Singh in Birmingham in 1992": https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-avinash-sable-india-athlete-steeplechase]
- 13:25.65: Avinash Sable, 2022, Sound Running meet, San Juan Capistrano (USA). [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-avinash-sable-india-athlete-steeplechase]
- 13:19.30: Avinash Sable, 6 May 2023, Sound Running On Track Fest, Los Angeles. [same Olympics.com URL; date from https://worldathletics.org/athletes/india/avinash-mukund-sable-14778911]
- 13:18.92: Gulveer Singh, June 2024, Portland Track Festival. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-gulveer-singh-india-athlete-long-distance-runner]
- 13:11.82: Gulveer Singh, 28 Sep 2024, Yogibo Athletics Challenge Cup, Niigata (Japan). [same URL; date from WIKI-NR]
- 12:59.77 (short track): Gulveer Singh, 21 Feb 2025, Boston. He was the first Asian to break 13 minutes indoors, and it is also an Asian record. [same URL; World Athletics profile]
- 13:03.93 (outdoor): Gulveer Singh, 24 May 2026 (IST; 23 May local US date per WIKI-NR), Sound Running Track Fest, Los Angeles. He finished 2nd behind Habtom Samuel (12:57.22). [https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/sports/with-a-silver-at-us-event-indian-army-athlete-breaks-5000-metre-national-record/]
- Records before 1992 (1970s–80s): **UNVERIFIED**.

### 10,000 m
- 28:48.72: Hari Chand, 1976 Montreal Olympics heats, run barefoot. It stood for 32 years. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Chand; https://www.olympics.com/en/news/hari-chand-indian-athlete-runner-olympian-dies]. Conflict: Olympics.com prints "29:48.72". That is probably a typo, because a slower time could not have been the record for 32 years. The NNIS obituary post and Wikipedia both give 28:48.72.
- 28:02.89: Surendra Singh, 2008, Vigo (Spain). [https://www.hindustantimes.com/sports/others/after-asian-games-10-000m-silver-kartik-targets-national-record-101697292334456.html]
- 27:41.81: Gulveer Singh, March 2024, The TEN, California. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/gulveer-singh-india-10000m-national-record-the-ten-2025-athletics-california]
- 27:14.88: Gulveer Singh, Nov 2024, Hachioji Long Distance, Japan. [same URL; https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-gulveer-singh-india-athlete-long-distance-runner]
- 27:00.22: Gulveer Singh, 29 Mar 2025, The TEN, San Juan Capistrano. He finished 6th, and the time makes him the "3rd fastest Asian of all time" according to SAI. [same Olympics.com URL; https://worldathletics.org/athletes/india/gulveer-singh-15023839]

### 3000 m steeplechase
- 8:30.88: Gopal Saini, 5 Jun 1981, Asian Championships, Tokyo. It stood for 37 years. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Saini (Wikipedia); corroborated by https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-avinash-sable-india-athlete-steeplechase]
- Avinash Sable then broke the record 10 times. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-avinash-sable-india-athlete-steeplechase; https://www.indiatoday.in/sports/other-sports/story/avinash-sable-shatters-national-record-for-10th-time-ahead-of-paris-olympics-2563569-2024-07-07]
  - 8:29.80: 28 Sep 2018, Open Nationals, Bhubaneswar. Olympics.com calls the meet "Federation Cup" in its table and gives the time as 8:29.88 in its text, which is an internal inconsistency; Wikipedia gives 8:29.80 at the National Open.
  - 8:28.94: 18 Mar 2019, Federation Cup, Patiala.
  - 8:25.23: 1 Oct 2019, World Championships heats, Doha.
  - 8:21.37: 4 Oct 2019, World Championships final, Doha.
  - 8:20.20: 17 Mar 2021, Federation Cup.
  - 8:18.12: 30 Jul 2021, Tokyo Olympics heats.
  - 8:16.21: 23 Mar 2022, Indian Grand Prix.
  - 8:12.48: 5 Jun 2022, Rabat Diamond League.
  - 8:11.20: 6 Aug 2022, Commonwealth Games, Birmingham.
  - 8:09.91: 7 Jul 2024, Paris Diamond League.

### Half marathon
- 1:00:30: Avinash Sable, 29 Nov 2020, Delhi Half Marathon. He was the first Indian under 61 minutes. [https://worldathletics.org/athletes/india/avinash-mukund-sable-14778911; WIKI-NR citing ESPN]
- 59:42: Gulveer Singh, 15 Mar 2026, NYC Half Marathon, 3rd place. Because the course is point-to-point, the time is not record-eligible, so Sable's 1:00:30 remains the official record. [OLY-NR; WIKI-NR]
- Records before 2020: **UNVERIFIED**.

### Marathon
- 2:12:00: Shivnath Singh, 28 May 1978, National Inter-State Championship, Jalandhar. It stood for about 48 years and was the longest-standing Indian athletics record. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/sawan-barwal-breaks-marathon-national-record-india; the date comes from WIKI-NR]
- 2:11:58: Sawan Barwal, 12 Apr 2026, Rotterdam Marathon, 20th place, on his marathon debut. [same Olympics.com URL; Olympics.com writes "2:11.58s" and "0.42 seconds", which is a formatting error, and the true margin is 2 seconds]
- 2:11:37: Sawan Barwal, 26 Sep 2026, Asian Games, Nagoya, silver medal. [https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/sports/who-is-sawan-barwal-himachal-runner-who-challenged-marathon-myth-to-win-asian-games-silver/; OLY-NR]

## 3. Athlete profiles

### Shivnath Singh (1946–2003)
- Born 11 Jul 1946 in Majharia, Buxar district, Bihar. He joined the Indian Army in the Bihar Regiment, rose to Naib Subedar, and died on 6 Jun 2003 in Jamshedpur. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivnath_Singh (Wikipedia only; the article links obituaries in The Hindu, TOI and Rediff)]
- Events: 5000 m, 10,000 m and marathon. He is reported to have run barefoot for his whole career. [Wikipedia; https://thecronica.substack.com/p/a-barefoot-record-of-an-indian-marathoner]
- Marathon PB and former national record: 2:12:00, Jalandhar, 1978. The record lasted 48 years. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/sawan-barwal-breaks-marathon-national-record-india]
- 1976 Montreal Olympic marathon: 11th. [Wikipedia; also a Facebook repost]. The time is **UNVERIFIED**: Wikipedia gives 2:16:22 and a Wire article snippet says 2:15:58.
- 1980 Moscow Olympic marathon: did not finish. [Wikipedia only]
- Asian Championships silvers in the 5000 m and 10,000 m in both 1973 (Marikina) and 1975 (Seoul). He lost the 1975 10,000 m to Hari Chand. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivnath_Singh; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Chand]
- Asian Games 1974 5000 m gold: Wikipedia categories only, so **UNVERIFIED**.
- After leaving the Army he worked at TISCO in Jamshedpur. [https://thecronica.substack.com/p/a-barefoot-record-of-an-indian-marathoner (blog)]
- Coach and training base: **UNVERIFIED**.
- Doping: none found.

### Hari Chand (1953–2022)
- Born 1 Apr 1953. He died on 13 Jun 2022 in Hoshiarpur district, Punjab, aged 69. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/hari-chand-indian-athlete-runner-olympian-dies]. TOI gives his village as Ghorewaha, Hoshiarpur, and says he died in a Jalandhar hospital: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/more-sports/athletics/olympian-hari-chand-who-created-10000m-national-record-barefoot-in-montreal-dies-unsung/articleshow/92183859.cms. Wikipedia instead spells the village "Chorewala".
- Service: he joined the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) in July 1972 and served as an inspector; he retired as a CRPF Commandant. [Olympics.com above mentions "inspector for the CRPF"; Wikipedia gives the details; the "retired as Commandant" point is from an NNIS social post]
- Events: 5000 m and 10,000 m, plus the marathon in 1980. [Olympics.com]
- 1975 Asian Championships, Seoul: gold in the 10,000 m and bronze in the 5000 m. He received the Arjuna Award for these. [Olympics.com]
- 1976 Montreal Olympics: ran barefoot and finished 8th in his 10,000 m heat. His time was the national record for 32 years (see the time conflict in section 2). [Olympics.com; Wikipedia]
- 1978 Asian Games, Bangkok: gold in both the 5000 m and 10,000 m. [Olympics.com]
- 1980 Moscow Olympics: went out in the 10,000 m heats. [Olympics.com]. He also finished 22nd in the marathon (Wikipedia only).
- He missed the 1982 Asian Games because of a viral fever. [Olympics.com]
- Coach and training base: **UNVERIFIED**.

### Thonakal Gopi (T. Gopi)
- Born 24 May 1988 in Wayanad, Kerala. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thonakal_Gopi (Wikipedia only for the date)]
- Background: from Sulthan Bathery, Wayanad, which sits about 1000 m above sea level. His farmer parents grew rice and ginger. He joined the Army through the sports quota and is a Havildar in the Artillery regiment. [https://www.espn.com/athletics/story/_/id/17475379/india-marathon-runner-olympics-thonackal-gopi-talks-espn-exclusive-interview]. The Indian Army lists him as "Havildar Gopi Thonakal … Army Sports Institute (ASI) Pune". [https://www.facebook.com/Indianarmy.adgpi/posts/havildar-gopi-thonakal-of-indianarmy-army-sports-institute-asi-pune-won-the-16th/736997223163576/]
- Events: 10,000 m and marathon.
- Marathon PB: 2:12:23 on 7 Dec 2025 at the Valencia Marathon, 23 seconds outside the old record. His previous PB was 2:13:39 (Seoul, 2019). [https://shyamgopan.com/2025/12/11/gopi-23-secs-short-of-national-record-sets-new-pb-at-valencia-marathon/; https://www.indiasportshub.com/articles/gopi-thonakals-valencia-marathon-run-rekindles-indian-marathon-hope]
- 2016 Rio Olympic marathon: 25th in 2:15:25, then a PB. [https://www.firstpost.com/sports/rio-olympics-2016-thanackal-gopi-and-kheta-ram-clock-personal-best-to-finish-25th-26th-in-marathon-2969066.html]
- 2017 Asian Marathon Championship, Dongguan: gold in 2:15:48, the first Indian to win the title. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/asian-marathon-championships-2024-india-results]
- 2017 Asian Championships, Bhubaneswar: silver in the 10,000 m. 2016 South Asian Games: 10,000 m gold. 2017 World Championships, London: marathon, 28th. [Wikipedia only]
- 2018 Mumbai Marathon: won in 2:16:51, 0.3 s ahead of Nitendra Rawat. [https://scroll.in/field/865807/mumbai-marathon-on-the-road-to-redemption-nitendra-singh-rawat-puts-up-a-stellar-performance]
- 2026 Rotterdam Marathon: 23rd in 2:13:16, inside the AFI Asian Games standard of 2:15:04. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/sawan-barwal-breaks-marathon-national-record-india]
- Coach: Surendra Singh Bhandari, Army Sports Institute Pune, before Rio. [ESPN above]
- Altitude training:
  - Before Rio, a "ten-month training stint at a high-altitude Ooty facility". [ESPN]
  - Before Valencia 2025, four months at Ooty, and Bengaluru has been his base for the last six years. [shyamgopan.com above]
  - Quote: "I think the high altitude at Ooty helped my performance." [https://shyamgopan.com/2025/12/11/gopi-23-secs-short-of-national-record-sets-new-pb-at-valencia-marathon/]
- Doping: none found.

### Kheta Ram
- Born 20 Sep 1986 in Khokhsar, Barmer district, Rajasthan. He grew up in the desert and ran 4 km to school. He joined the Army at 18 and is a Naib Subedar in the Jat Regiment. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kheta_Ram (Wikipedia only)]
- 2016 Rio Olympic marathon: 26th in 2:15:26, a PB, one second behind Gopi. [https://www.firstpost.com/sports/rio-olympics-2016-thanackal-gopi-and-kheta-ram-clock-personal-best-to-finish-25th-26th-in-marathon-2969066.html; ESPN URL above]
- 2014 Asian Games 5000 m: 7th. 2013 Asian Championships 10,000 m: 4th. 2016 South Asian Games marathon: bronze. [Wikipedia only]
- Coach and training: Surendra Singh Bhandari's Ooty altitude block before Rio, which also covered Gopi and Rawat. [ESPN URL above]. Wikipedia adds the Army Sports Institute Pune, Wellington/Coonoor in the Nilgiris, and SAI Bangalore (Wikipedia only).
- Doping: none found.

### Nitendra Singh Rawat
- Born 29 Sep 1986 in Garur, Bageshwar district, Uttarakhand. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitendra_Singh_Rawat (Wikipedia only)]
- Army runner. [https://www.sportskeeda.com/athletics/news-nitendra-singh-rawat-qualifies-hangzhou-asian-games]
- Marathon PB: 2:15:48 on 17 Jan 2016 at the Mumbai Marathon. This set the Indian course record, beating Ram Singh Yadav's 2:16:59, and qualified him for Rio. [https://sportskeeda.com/running/nitendra-rawat-credits-coach-success-mumbai-marathon (IANS)]
- 2016 Rio Olympic marathon: 84th in 2:22:52, running with a hamstring injury. [TOI: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/rio-2016-olympics/india-in-olympics-2016/athletics/rio-olympics-indias-thanackal-gopi-kheta-ram-clock-personal-best-in-marathon/articleshow/53800667.cms; https://scroll.in/field/865807/mumbai-marathon-on-the-road-to-redemption-nitendra-singh-rawat-puts-up-a-stellar-performance]
- 2016 South Asian Games marathon gold. [Wikipedia only]
- 2018 Mumbai Marathon: 2nd in 2:16:54. [Scroll above]
- 2022 national marathon title in 2:16:04. Wikipedia lists him at the 2022 Commonwealth Games (category only, **UNVERIFIED**). [Sportskeeda 2022 above]
- Coaches: Surinder Singh Bhandari at the national camp in Ooty (2016). Later, Army coach K.C. Ramu (2022). [Sportskeeda 2016 and 2022 URLs above]
- Quote: "I was personally confident after the training we had been put through in the national camp at Ooty by our coach (Surinder Singh) Bhandari." [https://sportskeeda.com/running/nitendra-rawat-credits-coach-success-mumbai-marathon]
- After Rio he recovered and trained with the national camp in Bangalore. [Scroll]
- Doping: none found.

### Govindan Lakshmanan (G. Lakshmanan, Tamil Nadu)
- Born 5 Jun 1990. [Wikipedia only]
- Background: from Sokkurani, Pudukkottai district, Tamil Nadu, "born in a poor family". He serves in the Army. His first trainer was S. Loganathan of the Kavinadu Youth Sports Club, where he trained on tar roads and village streets. [https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/Tiruchirapalli/athelets/article19251278.ece]. Wikipedia adds that he lost his father at six and was adopted by Loganathan (Wikipedia only).
- Coach: Surender Singh, his coach in 2017. [The Hindu above]
- 2015 Asian Championships, Wuhan: bronze in the 5000 m and silver in the 10,000 m. [The Hindu]
- 2017 Asian Championships, Bhubaneswar: gold in both the 5000 m and 10,000 m. This qualified him for the 2017 London World Championships. [The Hindu]
- PB: 13:35.69 in the 5000 m, London 2017. [Wikipedia only]
- 2018 Asian Games, Jakarta, 10,000 m: finished 3rd but was disqualified for a lane infringement (Rule 163.3b), and India's appeal was rejected. [https://scroll.in/field/891979/asian-games-2018-athletics-g-lakshmanan-disqualified-after-finishing-third-in-10000-metres-final; https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ani/asian-games-2018-lakshmanan-s-disqualification-to-stay-india-lose-bronze-118082600667_1.html]
- Doping: none found.

### Avinash Mukund Sable
- Born 13 Sep 1994 in Mandwa village, Beed district, Maharashtra. His parents were farmers, and he ran 6 km to school. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-avinash-sable-india-athlete-steeplechase; World Athletics profile]
- Army career: he joined after Class 12 in 5 Mahar Regiment, with postings at Siachen, Rajasthan and Sikkim. He took up running through Army cross country in 2015. He is an Army havildar; Wikipedia says he is now a Subedar. [Olympics.com above]
- Events: 3000 m steeplechase, 5000 m and half marathon.
- PBs [https://worldathletics.org/athletes/india/avinash-mukund-sable-14778911]:
  - Steeplechase 8:09.91 (NR, 7 Jul 2024, Paris).
  - 5000 m 13:19.30 (6 May 2023).
  - Half marathon 1:00:30 (29 Nov 2020, the official NR).
  - 10 km road 30:36 (19 May 2019).
  - 25 km 1:15:17 (17 Dec 2017, Kolkata).
- Records: he holds the steeplechase NR and the official half marathon NR. He held the 5000 m NR from 2022 to 2024. [OLY-NR]
- Olympics:
  - Tokyo 2020: national record of 8:18.12 in the heats, but no final. [Olympics.com]
  - Paris 2024: reached the final, the first Indian to do so in this event, and finished 11th in 8:14.18. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/paris-2024-olympics-athletics-india-avinash-sable-3000m-steeplechase-result; https://www.firstpost.com/sports/olympics-2024-athletics-india-avinash-sable-13802218.html]
- World Championships:
  - 2019 Doha: reached the final after an AFI appeal and finished 13th in 8:21.37 (NR). [Olympics.com]
  - He also ran in 2022 and 2023. [Hindustan Times, "third world championships": https://www.hindustantimes.com/sports/others/steeplechaser-avinash-sable-gets-set-for-a-third-world-championships-101692209605101.html]
  - He missed Tokyo 2025 through injury. [https://m.rediff.com/sports/report/avinash-sable-triumphant-return-to-steeplechase-after-injury/20260910.htm]
- Commonwealth Games: 2022 steeplechase silver in 8:11.20 (NR). This was India's first steeplechase medal at the Games and the first non-Kenyan medal in the event since 1994. [Olympics.com]
- Asian Games:
  - 2023 Hangzhou: steeplechase gold in 8:19.50 (Games record) and 5000 m silver in 13:21.09. [Olympics.com]
  - 2026 Aichi-Nagoya: 4th in the steeplechase in 8:21.67 (season best). He was entered in the 5000 m but did not start. [https://www.thestatesman.com/sports/gulveer-singh-adds-1500m-silver-to-asian-games-haul-sable-finishes-fourth-1503644118.html; https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/sports/gulveer-singh-wins-third-medal-at-2026-asian-games-clinches-bronze-in-mens-5000m/]
- Asian Championships: 2019 Doha steeplechase silver. 2025 Gumi steeplechase gold in 8:20.92, India's first men's title in the event in 36 years. [Olympics.com]
- Coaches:
  - Army coach Amrish Kumar spotted him in 2017 and moved him to the steeplechase. [Olympics.com, citing Scroll]
  - Russian coach Nikolai Snesarev at the national camp; Sable left him in 2018 and went back to Amrish Kumar. [Olympics.com]
  - Scott Simmons in Colorado Springs from 2022. [https://www.theweek.in/news/sports/2022/06/10/sub-8-minute-timings-not-impossible-record-breaking-steeplechaser-avinash-sable.html; HT 2023 above]
  - Kalyan Chaudhary (named as his coach for speed work in 2026). [Rediff 2026 above]
- Altitude and heat:
  - Altitude training at Colorado Springs and St Moritz (Switzerland) before Budapest 2023, alongside Paul Chelimo. [HT 2023 above]
  - His early Army postings exposed him to Siachen cold and Rajasthan desert heat of up to 50 °C. [Olympics.com]
  - SAI Southern Centre, Bengaluru, before the 2025 World Championships and again for his 2026 comeback. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/avinash-sable-injury-comeback-asian-games-2026-steeplechase]
- Injury: ACL and meniscus injury to his right knee at the Monaco Diamond League in July 2025, followed by surgery. His first steeplechase back was 8:32.39 at the Indian Athletics Series Final in Sep 2026. He could not compete at the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games. [Rediff 2026; Olympics.com comeback article]
- Quote: "I think I might have rushed my return a bit, which caused the injury to recur." [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/avinash-sable-injury-comeback-asian-games-2026-steeplechase]
- Coach Scott Simmons, quoted: "His adaptation upon his return to altitude this year was almost instant." [HT 2023 above]
- Arjuna Award 2022. [Olympics.com]
- Doping: none found.

### Gulveer Singh
- Born 1 Jun 1998 in Sirsa village, Aligarh district, Uttar Pradesh, into a farming family. [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-gulveer-singh-india-athlete-long-distance-runner; the date is also on World Athletics]
- Army career: he joined through the sports quota in 2018 with The Grenadiers and rose to Junior Commissioned Officer (Wikipedia gives the rank as Naib Subedar). He moved from the Army Sports Institute to the SAI national camp. He was posted in Arunachal Pradesh and did patrolling duties at altitude. [Olympics.com]
- Events: 1500 m to half marathon.
- PBs:
  - 1500 m 3:36.72 at the 2026 Asian Games final. [https://www.thestatesman.com/sports/gulveer-singh-adds-1500m-silver-to-asian-games-haul-sable-finishes-fourth-1503644118.html]. This conflicts with Olympics.com's figure of 3:36.58, so the exact 1500 m PB is **UNVERIFIED**.
  - 3000 m 7:34.49 (NR).
  - 5000 m 12:59.77 short track (NR, Asian record) and 13:03.93 outdoor.
  - 10,000 m 27:00.22 (NR).
  - Half marathon 59:42 (NYC, 15 Mar 2026, 3rd).
  - 25 km 1:12:06 (Kolkata, 21 Dec 2025).
  - [Olympics.com profile; World Athletics profile; WIKI-NR]
- He was the first Indian under 60 minutes for the half marathon. [Olympics.com]
- Asian Championships: 2023 Bangkok 5000 m bronze. 2025 Gumi gold in both the 5000 m (13:24.77, a championship record) and the 10,000 m. [Olympics.com]
- Asian Games:

## 4. Common patterns (drawn from the facts above)

1. **The services, above all the Army, carry Indian distance running.**
   - Nearly every current and recent elite athlete here is an Army soldier: Gulveer (Grenadiers), Sable (Mahar Regiment), Kartik (Jat Regiment), Sawan (Army; Wikipedia says Corps of Engineers), Gopi (Artillery), Kheta Ram (Jat Regiment), Rawat, Man Singh, Lakshmanan, Abhishek Pal and Jinson (851 Light Regiment).
   - Historically, Shivnath Singh was Army (Bihar Regiment) and Hari Chand was CRPF (police).
   - Several joined mainly for a steady income or a sports-quota job, not a sports career: Gopi, Kheta Ram, Gulveer, Kartik and Sable.
   - Many discovered competitive running only after enlisting: Sable in 2015 through Army cross country, and Gulveer ("only after joining the army").
   - The Army Sports Institute in Pune is a recurring hub: Gopi, Kheta Ram, Gulveer, and Sawan (per Wikipedia). [ESPN; Army post; Olympics.com; Tribune]
2. **Rural, farming and low-income backgrounds, often from hilly or harsh places.**
   - Farming families: Gopi, Sable, Gulveer, Kartik (sugarcane), Saroj and Lakshmanan.
   - Hill districts: Sawan (Mandi, HP), Man Singh (Pithoragarh), Rawat (Bageshwar), and Gopi (Wayanad, about 1000 m).
   - Desert: Kheta Ram (Barmer).
   - Long daily runs to school are reported for Sable (6 km) and Kheta Ram (4 km).
3. **Altitude training is standard.**
   - Domestic: the Nilgiris (Ooty and Wellington/Coonoor) is the main base. It served Surendra Singh Bhandari's Rio marathon group (Gopi, Kheta Ram, Rawat), Gopi before Valencia 2025, and Reliance's Project 2:09 group with Sawan (Nov–Feb). Bengaluru (SAI Southern Centre, ABSF) serves as the pre-competition and rehab base.
   - Foreign: from 2019 onward, Colorado Springs under the American Scott Simmons became the national distance hub. Athletes there include Jinson in 2019, Sable from 2022, Kartik in 2023, Gulveer from 2024, and Abhishek Pal in 2026. Sable also used St Moritz.
   - No documented Kenya/Iten or Bhutan camp was found for these athletes (**UNVERIFIED**).
4. **Heat and environmental extremes are mostly incidental, not planned.** Sable served in Siachen cold and 50 °C Rajasthan desert, and Kheta Ram grew up in the Barmer desert. I found no documented, deliberate heat-acclimation protocol.
5. **Coaching lineages.**
   - Army and national coaches: Surendra/Surinder Singh Bhandari (marathon), Amrish Kumar (Sable), K.C. Ramu (Rawat), and Nikolai Snesarev (the Russian national distance coach until about 2019).
   - After that, the US-based Scott Simmons.
   - A private marathon program: Reliance Foundation under Ajith Markose (Sawan).
6. **Record breakthroughs came from racing abroad.**
   - Almost every record since 2022 was set at overseas meets with fast pacing: The TEN in California, Sound Running in LA, Portland, Niigata, Hachioji, Boston indoor, Budapest, Paris Diamond League, Rotterdam and NYC.
   - This matches Sable's point that no one in India could set record pace (as reported by ESPN via Olympics.com).
   - The long-standing marks were 1978 (marathon), 1976 (10,000 m), 1981 (steeplechase) and 1992 (5000 m). They fell only in the late-2010s to 2020s era of foreign coaching and racing.
7. **Doping risk around the US hub.** Two Simmons-group athletes have anti-doping cases:
   - Kartik Kumar: a proven USADA 3-year ban for anabolic steroids (2025).
   - Abhishek Pal: a NADA clomiphene AAF (2026), still pending and not proven.
   - The Tribune explicitly notes that both trained under Simmons. No violation was found for Gulveer, Sable, Sawan, Gopi, Man Singh, Jinson or the historical athletes.
