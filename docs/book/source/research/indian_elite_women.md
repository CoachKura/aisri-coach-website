Notes on sources:

- Wikipedia is used only as a pointer. Where only Wikipedia supports a fact, it is labelled "(Wikipedia only)".
- Direct quotes are copied from the cited article only.

## 1. Sunita Rani (1500 m / 5000 m)
- **Born and background:** Born 4 Dec 1979 in Sunam, Sangrur district, Punjab [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunita_Rani] [https://sunitaraniathlete.com/about/]
- **Family:** Her father, Ram Sarup (Ramswaroop), was a village patwari (revenue official). As a schoolgirl she walked or ran 5 km to school [https://www.bhaskar.com/news/this-lady-ips-became-an-athlete-found-padma-shri-5862935.html]
- **Job:** Joined Punjab Police through the sports quota, as DSP in 2002 and SP in 2010. She was later posted as SP in Pathankot [same Bhaskar URL]
- **Personal bests** (WA via Wikipedia):
  - 1500 m: 4:06.03 (Busan, 2002). This was the national record until 2021 [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunita_Rani] [https://www.olympics.com/en/news/who-is-harmilan-bains-india-athlete-medals-records]
  - 5000 m: 15:18.77 (Busan, 2002)
- **Major results:**
  - 2002 Asian Games: 1500 m gold and 5000 m bronze
  - 1998 Asian Games: 5000 m silver and 1500 m bronze (Wikipedia only) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunita_Rani]
- **Awards:** Arjuna Award (1999) and Padma Shri [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunita_Rani]
- **Anti-doping case:** She tested positive at Busan 2002 for a nandrolone metabolite and her medals were stripped. The IAAF then cleared her on 3 Jan 2003 after accepting that the Games laboratory's testing was unreliable, and her medals were restored. **This is not a sustained violation** [https://www.rediff.com/sports/2003/jan/03rani.htm]
- **Coach and altitude training:** **UNVERIFIED**.

## 2. O. P. Jaisha (1500 m → steeplechase → marathon)
- **Full name:** Orchatteri Puthiyaveetil Jaisha
- **Born and background:** Born 23 May 1983 in Kerala. Studied at Assumption College, Changanassery [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._P._Jaisha]
- **Personal best:** Marathon 2:34:43, national record, Beijing, 30 Aug 2015 (18th at the World Championships) [http://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/sports/more-sports/athletics/World-Athletics-Championships-Indias-Jaisha-Orchatteri-breaks-national-record-in-womens-marathon/articleshow/48728686.cms]
- **Earlier national records:**
  - Steeplechase: 10:03.05, Patiala, 7 Aug 2010. She had taken up the event only in 2008 (Wikipedia citing The Hindu) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._P._Jaisha]
  - Marathon: 2:37:29 at the Mumbai Marathon, Jan 2015, on her debut [https://www.sportskeeda.com/running/o-p-jaisha-karan-singh-winners-mumbai-marathon]
- **Major results:**
  - 2006 Asian Games: 5000 m bronze
  - 2014 Asian Games: 1500 m bronze (4:13.46)
  - 2005 Asian Indoor Games: 1500 m and 3000 m gold
  - 2006 Commonwealth Games (Melbourne): competed
  - 2016 Rio Olympic marathon: 89th [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._P._Jaisha] [https://sportskeeda.com/athletics/rio-olympics-2016-athletics-op-jaisha-kavita-raut-put-up-dismal-show-womens-marathon-event]
- **Coach:** Nikolai Snesarev, the Belarusian national middle- and long-distance coach [https://www.sportskeeda.com/running/o-p-jaisha-karan-singh-winners-mumbai-marathon] [https://scroll.in/field/814706/indian-marathon-runner-kavita-raut-seems-to-contradict-compatriot-s-version-about-callous-officials]
- **Rio 2016 dispute:** After collapsing at the finish, Jaisha said Indian officials gave her no refreshments. Scroll quotes her: "All the [other] countries had their stalls at every two kilometres, but our country's stall was empty." The AFI disputed this, and Kavita Raut said she had enough water [https://scroll.in/field/814706/indian-marathon-runner-kavita-raut-seems-to-contradict-compatriot-s-version-about-callous-officials]
- **Training bases and altitude:** **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Anti-doping:** None found.

## 3. Lalita Babar (steeplechase, marathon)
- **Full name:** Lalita Shivaji Babar
- **Born and background:** Born 2 Jun 1989 in Mohi village, Satara district, Maharashtra, into a farming family in a drought-prone area [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalita_Babar] [https://thebridge.in/athletics/lalita-babar-feels-proud-by-making-her-village-girls-established-athletes]
- **Childhood:** As a child she ran 4 km each way to school and fetched water for a 17-person household. She earned her first prize money, Rs 10,000, by winning a 10 km road race at 15 in 2004 [https://www.thequint.com/sports/sports-buzz/from-running-in-fields-to-the-rio-tracks-lalita-babars-story]
- **Job:** Indian Railways employee, then set to join the Maharashtra government as a deputy collector (per a report from about 2019) [https://thebridge.in/athletics/lalita-babar-feels-proud-by-making-her-village-girls-established-athletes]. Her exact Railways post (TC/CTI) is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Personal bests:**
  - Steeplechase: 9:19.76, national record at the time, Rio Olympic heat, Aug 2016 [http://indianexpress.com/sports/rio-2016-olympics/lalita-babar-india-3000m-steeplechase-result-final-medal-2973964/]
  - Marathon: 2:38:21, Mumbai 2015 [https://www.sportskeeda.com/running/o-p-jaisha-karan-singh-winners-mumbai-marathon]
  - 10 km road: 34:14, Hyderabad, 28 Nov 2010, a national record per Wikipedia citing AIMS [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_records_in_athletics]. **CONFLICT:** Kavita Raut's WA profile shows a 32:54 10 km road mark (asterisked), so the "NR" status is doubtful.
- **Major results:**
  - 2014 Asian Games: steeplechase bronze
  - 2015 Asian Championships: steeplechase gold (9:34.13)
  - 2015 World Championships: 8th in the final, the first Indian woman to reach a steeplechase final
  - 2016 Rio Olympics: 10th in the final (9:22.74), the first Indian to reach an Olympic track final since P. T. Usha in 1984
  - Won the Mumbai Marathon three times in a row before 2015
  - Sources: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalita_Babar] [http://indianexpress.com/sports/rio-2016-olympics/lalita-babar-finishes-10th-in-3000m-steeplechase-india-2977275/] [https://thebridge.in/athletics/india-athletics-coach-nikolai-snesarev-dead-19834]
- **Coach:** Nikolai Snesarev. He introduced her to the steeplechase around 2010, and she switched to it fully in 2014. Eight months later she won the Asian Games bronze [https://www.thequint.com/sports/sports-buzz/from-running-in-fields-to-the-rio-tracks-lalita-babars-story] [https://thebridge.in/athletics/india-athletics-coach-nikolai-snesarev-dead-19834]
- **Training base:** In 2016 she trained in a Bengaluru camp with Sudha Singh under Snesarev [https://www.hindustantimes.com/other-sports/national-record-a-gift-for-india-s-support-steeplechaser-sudha-singh/story-zUIqWcXotQy21aqUfvaiXK.html]. Ooty altitude training appeared only in a search-engine summary and is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Quote** (The Bridge): "My parents inspired me, continuously pushing me to devote my life to athletics after having watched my passion." [https://thebridge.in/athletics/lalita-babar-feels-proud-by-making-her-village-girls-established-athletes]
- **Anti-doping:** None found.

## Common patterns (drawn from the facts above)

1. **Rural, low-income roots.** Most of these runners come from villages or small towns: Mohi (Lalita), Sawarpada (Kavita), Iklauta (Parul), Maroda (Ankita), Reta (Seema), Sangwan (Poonam), Sunam (Sunita), Idukki (Preeja). Most come from farming, labouring or low-grade government-job families, and several lost a parent young (Preeja and Seema). Daily hard running or walking to school or for water is a recurring origin story (Lalita, Kavita, Sunita).
2. **A job is the professional contract.** Railways (Sudha, Lalita, Preeja, Parul, Pooja Olla, and reportedly Chinta), police (Sunita, Parul) and PSUs such as ONGC (Kavita) pay these athletes. Jyoti Gawate, who never got a job, shows the alternative: living on prize money and running a heavy race schedule without access to the national camp.
3. **Clusters around a coach or region.**
   - Snesarev's national group (2005–10 and 2014–16): Preeja, Kavita, Sudha, Lalita, Jaisha. It produced the 2010 Asian Games 10,000 m 1–2, the steeplechase gold, and the 2015–16 records.
   - Vijender Singh's Nashik group: Kavita, Monika, Sanjivani, and possibly Poonam.
   - The Colorado Springs group under Scott Simmons (ADP): Parul, and Seema's 2026 preparation.
4. **Altitude and overseas bases replaced domestic-only preparation after about 2018.** Examples: Colorado Springs (Parul); the Bhopal EDRP with annual Iten, Kenya trips (Ankita's group); US track meets for fast marks (Deeksha, Sanjivani, Seema, Parul all set bests in California or Oregon); Ooty altitude blocks (Priti). Earlier generations mostly used domestic camps (Bengaluru, Patiala). Ankita's native village is itself at about 1400 m.
5. **Records cluster in rare peak events.** The 10,000 m (2010), half marathon (2017) and marathon (2015) records have stood 9–16 years. Recent gains are concentrated in steeplechase and 5000 m, almost entirely Parul Chaudhary: six national records from 2022 to 2026.
6. **Event switching is common and often successful.**
   - Road → steeplechase: Lalita
   - 1500 m/steeplechase → marathon: Jaisha
   - Steeplechase → marathon for Worlds qualification: Sudha
   - 800 m → 5000 m/steeplechase: Parul
   - Wrestling → distance running: Sanjivani
   - Hockey → middle distance: Pooja Olla
7. **Anti-doping.** One sustained violation in this group:
   - Sanjivani Jadhav: 2-year AIU ban (2019), probenecid, and loss of her 2019 Asian Championships bronze.
   - Sunita Rani's 2002 positive was overturned and her medals restored.
   - No other clearly sourced violations were found for the listed athletes.
